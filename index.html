AI;A Human Guide

How can it help? What can go wrong? What could the future look like? Extra Reading:

AI is becoming part of everyday life. People use it to answer questions, organise information, create content and complete routine tasks. It is changing how we learn, work and communicate, so it is important to understand both its capabilities and its limitations. Although AI can be impressive, its accuracy depends on the task and the information available to it. One main idea should remain central: AI is a useful assistant, not a reason to stop thinking critically.

Artificial intelligence is software that uses information to produce predictions, decisions or new content. Many modern AI systems learn patterns from examples instead of having every rule written by a programmer.

Generative AI goes further by producing text, images or audio from patterns it has learned. A chatbot generates a response piece by piece, creating language that can sound natural and convincing. However, a confident answer is not necessarily a correct one. Users still need to consider where the information comes from and whether there is evidence to support it.

How can it help?

AI can explain a topic in simpler terms or create quizzes to help students practise. It can also suggest examples or explain an idea in a different way. However, students should compare its answers with reliable course materials and avoid allowing it to replace their own effort.

AI can help draft an email, organise ideas or summarise information, saving time for other important tasks. For example, it could turn a set of meeting notes into a first draft of an action list. A person should still check that the summary is accurate and that nothing important has been left out.

AI can help with translations, making international communication easier and more accessible. Tools that turn speech into text can also support captions and written records. However, mistakes with language, accents or context can change the meaning of a message.

These are some possible uses, but results still require checking.

What can go wrong?

Accuracy:

AI can give incorrect information or invent facts and sources. These mistakes, often called ‘hallucinations’, can sound convincing, so important claims require checking. For example, a chatbot might suggest a book or article that does not exist. Users should open the original sources rather than assuming that a reference is genuine.

Bias:

AI can repeat unfair patterns in the information it learns from. This could lead to unfair treatment, for example, when reviewing job applications. If the examples used to develop a system reflect past discrimination, its recommendations may repeat those problems. Human oversight and checks for unfair outcomes are therefore important.

Privacy:

Sharing personal or confidential information with an AI tool can put that information at risk. Users should be careful about uploading details such as passwords, financial information or private documents.

Deepfakes and fraud:

AI can create realistic fake images, videos and voices. These can be used to spread misinformation or impersonate someone. An unexpected message or familiar-sounding voice should not automatically be trusted, especially if it requests money or sensitive information.

Dependence:

Relying too heavily on AI may reduce opportunities to practise skills and think independently. It is important to use AI as support while keeping responsibility for the final result. People should also be able to continue essential tasks if a tool becomes unavailable.

What could the future look like?

AI could offer new opportunities in education, research and public services. However, improvements in technology will need to be supported by clear responsibilities and human supervision. People should know who checks important decisions and how mistakes can be challenged or corrected.

AI also depends on physical infrastructure, including computers, internet connections and electricity. Growing demand from data centres can put pressure on local power systems. Blackouts are not an inevitable result of AI, but reliable electricity supplies and careful planning matter. Service outages and cyberattacks are further reasons to maintain backup plans and alternatives.

Ultimately, the future of AI will depend on the choices people make. Using it responsibly means recognising its benefits while remaining willing to question its answers, protect private information and keep people involved in important decisions.
